A little clue: These verbs have irregular present-tense forms. Learn each verb with its subject.

3. The Rules

1. Match the verb to the person

The verb form changes with the subject. All the examples in this lesson use the present tense.

Learn forms in this order: yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros/nosotras, vosotros/vosotras, ellos/ellas/ustedes.

Usted means formal “you” but uses the same verb form as él and ella. Ustedes means “you all” and uses the same form as ellos and ellas. Vosotros/vosotras is informal plural “you,” mainly used in Spain.

2. Ser: identity and characteristics

Use ser for identity, origin, characteristics, and time.

Soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son.

Yo soy de México. — I am from Mexico.

Ella es profesora. — She is a teacher.

3. Oír: hearing

Oír means “to hear.” The yo form is oigo; several other forms use y.

Oigo, oyes, oye, oímos, oís, oyen.

Yo oigo música. — I hear music.

Keep the written accents in oír, oímos, and oís.

4. Estar: states and locations

Use estar for states, conditions, and the location of people or things.

Estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están.

Ella está en casa. — She is at home.

Ser and estar both mean “to be,” but their uses differ. Avoid a simple permanent/temporary rule: Madrid está en España describes a lasting location. Events use ser for location: La fiesta es en casa. — The party is at home.

5. Ir: going somewhere

Ir means “to go.” Learn its present-tense forms together.

Voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van.

Use a before a destination: Voy a la escuela. — I go to school.

A + el becomes al: Voy al parque. — I go to the park.

6. Tener: having

The yo form is tengo. The stem changes from e to ie in tienes, tiene, and tienen.

Tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen.

Yo tengo un libro. — I have a book.

Spanish also uses tener for age: Tengo diez años. — I am ten years old.

7. Decir: saying or telling

The yo form is digo. The stem changes from e to i in dices, dice, and dicen.

Digo, dices, dice, decimos, decís, dicen.

Nosotros decimos la verdad. — We tell the truth.

8. Subjects and negatives

You can omit the subject when it is clear: Tengo un libro. — I have a book. The conjugation quiz uses answers without subject pronouns.

For a negative statement, put no before the verb: No oigo música. — I do not hear music.

Practice the irregular forms as whole words, including their written accents.
